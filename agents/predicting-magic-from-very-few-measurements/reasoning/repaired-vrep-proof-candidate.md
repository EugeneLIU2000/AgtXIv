# Candidate repair of the reduced-polytope V-representation

Status: `AGENT_RECONSTRUCTED / PENDING_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW`.

This note repairs the gap recorded in `reviews/vrep-proof-gap.md`; it does not
claim that the source proof already contained the argument below.

## Normalized setting

Let `M` be a finite set of fixed Hermitian involutions representing distinct
nonidentity Pauli phase classes, with no duplicate or `±`-duplicate class.  For a
pure stabilizer state `σ`, every coordinate `tr(Pσ)` is in `{−1,0,+1}`.  Its
nonzero measured support is a commuting subset of `M`.  Let `G_M` join exactly
the anticommuting pairs.  For an inclusion-maximal independent set `S` of
`G_M`, let `B_S` contain exactly the sign functions compatible with all Pauli
product relations within `S`.  The candidate point `v_(S,f)` equals `f(P)` on
`S` and zero outside `S`.

We prove the two set inclusions and then prove that every listed point is
extreme.

## 1. Every listed maximal-context point is physical

Fix a maximal commuting context `S` and an admissible sign function `f`.
Choose `R_1,…,R_r` from `S` whose phase-free binary symplectic vectors are
independent and span the vectors of `S`.  Because the fixed representatives in
`S` commute and square to identity, every `P∈S` has a unique expression

`P = η_P ∏_j R_j^(a_j)`, with `η_P∈{−1,+1}` and `a_j∈F_2`.

Write `ε_j=f(R_j)`.  The relation between `P` and the `R_j`, together with the
definition of `B_S`, forces

`f(P)=η_P ∏_j ε_j^(a_j)`.

Conversely, every assignment `ε∈{−1,+1}^r` defines a unique `f` by this formula
and satisfies every phase-aware relation in `S`.  Thus `B_S` is in bijection
with the `2^r` independent-generator eigenvalue assignments.  Equivalently,
the signed generators `{ε_jR_j}` generate no `−I`.  This avoids treating `f`
as an ordinary character of the unsigned subgroup, which can contain `−I`.

The joint-eigenspace projector

`Π_f = 2^(−r) ∏_j (I + ε_j R_j)`

is a projector.  Phase-free independence makes every nonidentity Pauli in its
expansion traceless, hence `tr(Π_f)=2^(n−r)>0`.  Thus the joint eigenspace is
nonempty.  Extend the isotropic signed span generated by `{ε_jR_j}`—which does
not contain `−I`—to a rank-`n` stabilizer group and choose any pure joint
eigenstate `σ_(S,f)` in that eigenspace.  It has expectation `f(P)` for all
`P∈S`.

For every `R∈M\S`, maximality of `S` inside `M` supplies some `P∈S` that
anticommutes with `R`.  Since `σ_(S,f)` is a `P` eigenstate,

`tr(R σ_(S,f)) = tr(P R P σ_(S,f)) = −tr(R σ_(S,f)) = 0`.

Therefore its projection is exactly `v_(S,f)`.  Every listed point belongs to
`STAB(M)`.

## 2. Every stabilizer projection is a mixture of listed points

It suffices to consider a pure stabilizer state `σ`, because the full
stabilizer polytope is their convex hull.  Define

`C = {P∈M : |tr(Pσ)|=1}`.

The set `C` is commuting.  Choose an inclusion-maximal commuting context
`S⊇C` in `M`.  Use an independent basis `R_1,…,R_r` of `S` and its orthogonal
joint eigenspace projectors `Π_f`, indexed by the equivalent sign functions
`f∈B_S` established in Step 1.  They obey `Σ_f Π_f=I`.  Define
`p_f=tr(Π_fσ)`.  The weights are nonnegative and sum to one.  Step 1 separately
supplies a physical projected point `v_(S,f)` for every such label; no claim
about the postmeasurement state is needed.

For `P∈S`, the spectral identity `P=Σ_f f(P)Π_f` gives
`Σ_f p_ff(P)=tr(Pσ)`.  For `R∈M\S`, the original expectation is zero, because every
nonzero stabilizer expectation was placed in `C⊆S`; every outcome vector also
has zero in this coordinate by maximality of `S`.  Hence the outcome average
agrees with the complete projected vector of `σ`:

`b_M(σ) = Σ_f p_f v_(S,f)`.

Thus every pure stabilizer projection, including a projection with nonmaximal
nonzero support, lies in the convex hull of the listed maximal-context points.
Together with Step 1, this proves the claimed convex-hull equality.

## 3. Every listed point is an extreme point

Suppose

`v_(S,f) = Σ_k λ_k v_(S_k,f_k)`, with `λ_k>0` and `Σ_k λ_k=1`.

For every `P∈S`, the left coordinate is the saturated value `f(P)∈{−1,+1}`,
whereas every right coordinate is in `[−1,+1]`.  Equality of the convex
average forces every contributing point to have the same coordinate `f(P)`.
In particular `S⊆S_k` for every `k`.  Both `S` and `S_k` are
inclusion-maximal independent sets, so `S=S_k`, and the saturated coordinates
then give `f=f_k`.  The convex decomposition is trivial; `v_(S,f)` is extreme.

## What this repairs—and what remains

The source's false bijection between arbitrary stabilizer intersections and
maximal contexts is unnecessary.  A nonmaximal intersection is instead a
convex mixture of maximal-context joint-measurement outcomes.  The two-qubit
example `M={Z₁,X₂}`, `σ=|00><00|` illustrates the repair: `(1,0)` is the equal
mixture of `(1,1)` and `(1,−1)`.
