\subsection{Case 2608.05845: formulas, implementations, and numerical evidence}\label{case:contrib-05845}

This molecular-simulation slice exposes the difference between occurrence reduction and semantic reduction. The qualitative statements that two viscosity methods agree across three decades and that no finite-size effect was observed down to 250 molecules recur in abstract, results, and conclusion locations. Each set of three occurrences may be consolidated into one displayed lineage while all source spans remain available. This is the largest unambiguously safe compression mechanism in the slice.

The OrthoBoXY viscosity equation and the implemented estimator share a mathematical relation, but the latter specifies how directional diffusion estimates are averaged. The implementation is therefore a separate contribution operation. Absolute and relative error-propagation formulas are also distinct outputs and both require the covariance-neglect assumption. A verified algebraic derivation does not license dropping that assumption from the semantic envelope.

Numerically close table values cannot be merged. For acetone, the current-work OrthoBoXY estimate, an other-work Green--Kubo estimate, and an experimental value differ in attribution, uncertainty, and evidence role. A comparison view may align them in one row, but the row contains three claims and three provenance chains.

This case supports a contribution profile with \status{implements}, \status{computes}, \status{benchmarks}, and \status{observes}. A formula count alone would miss the principal empirical and engineering contributions; a result-table cell count would exaggerate them by treating every aligned scalar as an independent conceptual innovation.
