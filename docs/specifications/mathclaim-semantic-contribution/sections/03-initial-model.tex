\subsection{Candidate relation lattice}

The initial relation inventory separates operations that text models often collapse:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \status{same\_occurrence\_lineage}: repeated source occurrences represent one already-adjudicated claim lineage;
  \item \status{semantic\_equivalent}: complete proposition and envelope are equivalent under the stated context;
  \item \status{math\_equivalent\_envelope\_distinct}: verified mathematical components are equivalent, but attribution, modality, interpretation, evidence, or another envelope component differs;
  \item \status{strictly\_entails}: the source proposition entails the target but not conversely under the declared context;
  \item \status{specializes} and \status{generalizes}: the mathematical relation changes domain or assumptions;
  \item \status{depends\_on}: the source requires the target as a premise or construction input but is not logically entailed by the target alone;
  \item \status{derivable\_exposition}: the claim's complete mathematical content follows from displayed verified nodes, while its envelope contributes no query-relevant independent assertion;
  \item \status{contradicts}, \status{corrects}, and \status{incomparable}; and
  \item \status{unknown} or \status{blocked} when the prerequisites for a stronger decision are absent.
\end{enumerate}
Mathematical equivalence is deliberately weaker than semantic equivalence. Every relation is read as ``source relation target''; in particular, \status{depends\_on} points from the dependent claim to its prerequisite. Strict implication is directional and is not interchangeable with duplicate status.

\subsection{Initial safe-reduction rule}

For query $q$, a claim $c$ may be hidden behind representative set $D$ only if all of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=R\arabic*.]
  \item $c$ and every member of $D$ are immutable revisions with resolvable source anchors.
  \item An accepted grounding identifies the complete mathematical component of $c$, or the decision is limited to consolidation of occurrences already assigned to one lineage.
  \item Accepted mathematical relations establish that $D$ covers the grounded component under the same or a stronger declared assumption context.
  \item Every truth-relevant envelope element of $c$ is equivalent to content displayed in $D$, explicitly rendered in the view, or declared irrelevant by the query policy without being discarded from the receipt.
  \item Hiding does not erase an independently assessable attribution, proof method, empirical result, numerical value, uncertainty, interpretation, limitation, contradiction, or contribution operation requested by $q$.
  \item The receipt identifies $c$, $D$, the accepted relations and groundings, policy revision, and a deterministic reconstruction path.
\end{enumerate}
Failure of any rule yields \status{visible}, \status{unknown}, or \status{blocked}; it never causes deletion.

\subsection{Contribution operations}

A contribution unit relates current-work output claims to one or more explicit baseline claims:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=interface]
ContributionUnit {
  unit_id
  paper_revision
  output_claim_refs[]
  baseline_claim_refs[]
  baseline_declarations[]
  operation_types[]
  dimension_witnesses
  math_delta
  assumption_delta
  conclusion_delta
  proof_or_method_delta
  semantic_envelope_delta
  verification_and_evidence
  attribution
  uncertainty
  status
  assessor_or_method
  provenance
}
\end{lstlisting}

The initial operation vocabulary is multi-label:
\begin{center}
\status{introduces}, \status{proves}, \status{reproves}, \status{strengthens}, \status{weakens\_assumptions},
\status{generalizes}, \status{specializes}, \status{unifies}, \status{refutes}, \status{computes},
\status{observes}, \status{implements}, \status{benchmarks}, and \status{interprets}.
\end{center}
A paper may introduce an object and prove a theorem about it; one enum would lose that structure.

\subsection{No scalar contribution score}

The initial aggregate is a vector with declared units:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf C(P\mid B,g)=
(N_{\mathrm{new}},N_{\mathrm{strengthened}},N_{\mathrm{weakened}},N_{\mathrm{new\ proof}},
N_{\mathrm{empirical}},N_{\mathrm{interpretive}},R_{\mathrm{dependency}},V_{\mathrm{coverage}}),
\end{equation}
where $P$ is the paper revision, $B$ the baseline set, and $g$ the granularity profile. Counts refer to accepted contribution units or verified mathematical equivalence classes, never raw sentences. $R_{\mathrm{dependency}}$ is a declared ratio of reused accepted mathematical dependencies to all accepted mathematical dependencies in the selected contribution subgraph. $V_{\mathrm{coverage}}$ reports verified and evidence-assessed coverage by type; it is not confidence in importance.

The vector supports component-wise comparison only when baseline, granularity, domain profile, and assessed dimensions agree. Otherwise profiles are incomparable. A Pareto frontier may summarize a declared comparison set. A weighted scalar may be derived only when an external decision task supplies weights, calibration data, and error analysis. It must not be called scientific quality, truth, novelty, or impact.

\subsection{Hypotheses to test}

The gold slices test five hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item repeated occurrences can be consolidated safely without hiding their locators;
  \item verified mathematical equivalence alone is insufficient for semantic merging;
  \item verified implication can reduce expository views only when scope and envelope checks pass;
  \item contribution operations are more stable than raw claim counts but remain baseline-sensitive; and
  \item numerical, security, attribution, and interpretive claims prevent a mathematics-only quotient from becoming the sole paper representation.
\end{enumerate}
