\section{Computational-linguistics evidence and transfer limits}\label{sec:literature}

This review compares annotation contracts rather than treating all scientific-text tasks as interchangeable. A rhetorical zone, citation intent, paraphrase pair, natural-language inference label, document-level result tuple, and verified mathematical dependency answer different questions. The source findings below are separated from AgtXIv design inferences.

\subsection{What similarity, inference, and coreference do not establish}

The literature provides useful candidate generators but no general-purpose semantic-pruning certificate. ParaSCI demonstrates that scientific paraphrase data can be collected at scale, not that an embedding threshold preserves formal meaning. SciNLI demonstrates a domain-specific inference task, while its reported error rate and label design show why a classifier output cannot silently remove a source claim. SciERC demonstrates that joint coreference improves relation coverage, not that co-referring spans state one proposition. SciREX demonstrates the value of document-level tuples, but its end-to-end gap warns against treating automatically extracted salience as complete.

AgtXIv therefore uses three increasingly strict comparisons:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item lexical or embedding similarity proposes a pair;
  \item source-aware semantic review determines attribution, modality, condition, and scope compatibility;
  \item accepted \MathClaim{} relations certify mathematical equivalence or implication only for the grounded mathematical component.
\end{enumerate}
None of the three licenses deletion. They can justify a reversible view decision when the residual semantic envelope is also preserved.

\subsection{Contribution extraction is not contribution measurement}

Contribution taxonomies identify what authors present as contributions. They are valuable for locating claims and defining comparison dimensions. They do not provide a calibrated measure of magnitude, truth, originality, or importance. NLPContributions also shows that annotation variance rises sharply as curators move from sentences to fine-grained triples. ORKG comparisons show that standardization is relative to an aligned comparison schema. The Nature of NLP corpus shows that contribution statements can be classified reliably enough for field-level analysis, but its classes remain categorical.

The transferable conclusion is limited: a paper contribution should be represented as a source-grounded relation between current-work claims and an explicit baseline, with typed operations and uncertainty. A count of categories, claims, formulas, proof steps, citations, or graph nodes is not a contribution score unless an external task supplies a validated utility function.

\subsection{Mathematical verification changes the safe boundary}

General computational-linguistics systems infer entailment from text. The present study assumes a stronger input for one layer: source-aligned \MathClaim{} nodes and their relevant dependencies have passed the declared mathematical verification gate. This permits accepted mathematical equivalence, implication, and dependency to replace model similarity for the covered component. It does not verify:
\begin{itemize}
  \item physical applicability or empirical premises;
  \item approximations and numerical convergence outside the formal contract;
  \item causal or interpretive language;
  \item attribution, novelty, priority, or importance;
  \item completeness of the source claim inventory; or
  \item equality of two claims' residual semantic envelopes.
\end{itemize}
The architecture must therefore expose exactly which clauses a \MathClaim{} covers and retain everything else.
