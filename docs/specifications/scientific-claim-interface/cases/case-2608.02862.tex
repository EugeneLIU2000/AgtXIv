\label{case:2608-02862}

Anandan et al., ``Selection criteria for the compressible Euler equations: numerical study'' (arXiv:2608.02862v1), tests whether entropy-, energy-, defect-, and norm-based functionals distinguish dissipative weak solutions produced by four numerical families on a Kelvin--Helmholtz benchmark. \paragraph{Source statements.}
The source defines a DW solution by a conjunction of regularity, energy monotonicity, weak continuity and momentum equations, a Young-measure entropy inequality, and compatible Reynolds-stress and energy defects. It imports the convergence dichotomy for consistent stable approximations and the equivalence between strong convergence and vanishing Reynolds defect. It then operationalizes selection through the observables
\begin{align}
\mathcal I_{\widetilde S}(t) &= \int_\Omega \widetilde S(t,\mathbf x) \mathrm d\mathbf x, &
D_S(t) &= \mathcal I_{\widetilde S}(t)-\mathcal I_{\widetilde S}(0),\\
\mathcal I_{\widehat E}(t) &= \int_\Omega E(\widetilde\varrho,\widetilde{\mathbf m},\widetilde S)(t,\mathbf x) \mathrm d\mathbf x, &
\mathcal D_E(t) &= \mathcal I_{\widetilde E}(t)-\mathcal I_{\widehat E}(t).
\end{align}
The finite study reports convergence of the Ces\`aro-averaged density, but not raw density, for every tested method with $N\geq 1$. Its entropy criteria repeatedly favor DVBFV~0 and RKDG~0, with DVBFV~1 also favored; energy criteria do not yield one winner. At $t=10$, the combined functional $\mathcal A$ places DVBFV~0, RKDG~0, DGSEM~1, DVBFV~1, and DGSEM~2 in its top five. The authors interpret DVBFV~0, RKDG~0, and DVBFV~1 as the most consistent candidates and state that selection is sensitive to scheme-dependent numerical diffusion.

\paragraph{Difficult atomicity and source cases.}
The DW definition is one named source object but contains several independently testable clauses. Splitting it would improve logical atomicity while losing the fact that no clause alone defines a DW solution. The record therefore retains one composite formal-definition candidate and marks \texttt{single\_primary\_predication=false}. Conversely, the four entropy rankings are consolidated because the paper's synthesis quantifies over that functional family; their individual table evidence and discussion remain anchored.

The strongest convergence statement is approximation-dependent rather than a claim about a continuum solution in isolation. Its scope includes the successive-grid $L^1$ error, $t=10$, interpolation to the finest grid, a finite mesh sequence, the tested methods, and $N\geq1$. The interface must not normalize this to unrestricted convergence as $h\to0$. Likewise, ``dominates globally'' means at all reported sample times, not all real times.

The paper juxtaposes theorem-backed and empirical content in one paragraph. The implications from selection for every $\lambda>0$ to local entropy maximality and from maximality to weak-solution status are attributed to cited prior theorems; the oscillatory Kelvin--Helmholtz behavior is a current numerical observation. The retained deliberate composite therefore uses \texttt{AMBIGUOUS} attribution only at bundle level and states clause ownership explicitly. Promotion should either split the clauses or use typed theorem--observation and imported--current relations; it must not flatten the entire comparison into \texttt{CURRENT\_WORK} or upgrade the mismatch to a proof.

\paragraph{MathClaimIR boundaries.}
Explicit definitions, inequalities, discounted functionals, and biconditionals are suitable or partly suitable for MathClaimIR. Numerical rankings require typed method, polynomial degree, mesh, time, quadrature, and optimization-direction entities; a bare inequality loses their evidentiary regime. ``Physically reasonable'', ``maximally turbulent'', ``dominates'', and ``most consistent'' are author interpretations, not mathematical predicates without a source-defined operationalization. Imported convergence statements also need provenance and assumptions that are only summarized in this paper.

\paragraph{Interface pressures.}
This case requires: multi-span support for abstract--discussion--conclusion repetition; explicit knowledge attribution; theorem versus observation status; finite-resolution and quadrature scope; algorithm and hyperparameter identity; optimization direction; convergence-mode typing; composite named definitions; negative results; and access limitations. Numerical evidence must remain distinct from scientific acceptance: a method winning a sampled functional is not thereby the unique physical Euler solution.

\paragraph{Provisional verdict.}
The five-field accepted identity kernel can represent the admitted principal claims without a paper-specific ontology. Conditions, attribution, and modality remain first-class admission semantics and must appear in the statement; interface-pressure fields remain candidate-review metadata rather than accepted-core columns. The design is weakest for structured empirical evidence: tables, finite mesh sequences, algorithm variants, and repeated rankings are compressed into prose scopes. A future evidence layer should type experimental regimes and table cells without encoding a preferred selection criterion as accepted truth.
