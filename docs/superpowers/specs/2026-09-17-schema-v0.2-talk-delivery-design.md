## 1. Why this plan replaced the last one

The v0.1 plan was written on 2026-09-16 and was sound given what was known then. A parallel
gap analysis — six surveyor agents, five skeptics, each reading and running against the real
repository — then established three facts that dissolved it. The full record is
`2026-09-16-framework-gap-analysis-record.md`.

**The five rounds were never five rounds.** The one existing v0.1 Paper Agent example was
produced by a model reading the source end to end in a single interactive session and writing
`extraction/seed.json` by hand — 440,887 bytes, as its own `extraction_note` states. Then
`host/assemble.py` replayed that seed into the five-round folder. It calls no model and makes no
network request; re-run into a scratch directory it reproduced `records.json`, `claims.md`,
`validation.json` and every `rounds/*/{task,draft,records}.json` **byte-identically in 18.2
seconds**. The five "rounds" are five Python functions slicing one seed, and `rounds/*/draft.json`
is generated by the script rather than captured from a model.

**The dependency stage is a regular-expression scan.** `handoff.py` pins
`HANDLER='local-citations/1.0'`, its scan is regex over bytes, and its manifest asserts
`external_model_calls: 0`.

**The third operation was contractually unreachable.** `validate.py:168` requires a
`formalization-packet` on any `formalization.generate` Task; the packet's only producer needs
`frozen-scope`, `argument-snapshot`, `proof-plan` and `formal-environment`, of which one has no
producing operation at all. Two independent agents reproduced the refusal.

None of that was dishonest — every one of those boundaries is written down somewhere in the
repository. The problem is narrower and more interesting: **the v0.1 contract did not forbid
any of it.** A folder assembled from a hand-written seed satisfied the Task schema. A regex
scan satisfied `dependency.search`.

`schema v0.2` closes that gap by definition. Its first principle reads: *every executed research
task calls a model; replaying a manual seed, copying an old result, or scanning citations alone
does not constitute a new Agent execution.* This is the pivot, and it is also the strongest
section available in the talk — a project that found its own contract too permissive and
tightened it is more credible than one that never looked.

Two smaller corrections carried over. The deadline is **2026-09-22**, not 09-23; `MIGRATION.md`
caught this. And the claim "anyone can re-check on a laptop in 84 seconds" needed its qualifier:
a one-time Mathlib cache download of a few gigabytes precedes it. On this machine that cache is
11 GB, of which 10 GB is compiled Mathlib against 104 MB of Mathlib source, shared by all three
Lean projects.

### 2.3 The contract refuses faked success

| Attempt | Outcome |
|---|---|
| `NOT_STARTED`, `call: null`, reason given | accepted — the honest state when no model ran |
| `COMMITTED` with `call: null` | **refused** |
| `COMMITTED` with a call but `SOURCES: NOT_RUN` | **refused** |
| `NOT_STARTED` carrying `source_bindings` | **refused** |
| fewer than seven check layers | **refused** |

One constraint surfaced only by running it: `report: null` is admissible **only** with status
`NOT_RUN`. Every `PASS` or `FAIL` must point at an actual check-report blob — a layer cannot be
marked passing without evidence behind it.

## 3. The worked example

**arXiv:2607.26154v1, "Graph Theoretic Approach to Quantum Nonstabilizerness."** Chosen because
it is current research, because three fields meet in it, and because **its main theorem is not
proved and the system says exactly why**.

The upstream closure of `claim:closed-form-rom` is **29 nodes across nine layers and five
branches**. Layer 0 holds six external foundations drawn from quantum information, graph theory
and optimization; layer 8 is the paper's result.

```
layer 8   claim:closed-form-rom                                        [closed_form]
layer 7   claim:exact-graph-program                                    [optimization]
layer 6   claim:relaxation-exactness   claim:relaxed-mwis-dual
layer 5   claim:sign-alignment-identity ✅   claim:perfect-graph-antiblocker  (+2)
layer 4   claim:relaxed-affine-span   claim:pauli-active-dependency
layer 3   claim:mwis-definition ✅   claim:perfect-graph-definition ✅   (+5)
layer 2   claim:frustration-graph   claim:reduced-rom   claim:reduced-stabilizer-polytope
layer 1   claim:full-rom   claim:full-stabilizer-polytope   claim:pauli-window
layer 0   ⛔ root:perfect-graph-weighted-duality      [graph_theory]
          ⛔ root:varela-reduced-polytope             [reduced_polytope]
             foundation:finite-lp-strong-duality      [optimization]
             root:gottesman-stabilizer-formalism / veitch / howard-campbell
```

Three rungs carry a kernel-checked Lean declaration, and **they sit in the mathematics
branches** while feeding a quantum-information conclusion — cross-field reuse visible without
being asserted:

```
claim:sign-alignment-identity  → AgtXIv.Stabilizerness.max_abs_signed_sum
claim:mwis-definition          → AgtXIv.GraphFoundation.maxWeightIndependent
claim:perfect-graph-definition → AgtXIv.GraphFoundation.IsPerfect
```

Two of the six foundations are blocked, and the figure makes it obvious they are load-bearing.
`foundations/perfect-graph-weighted-duality.json` records a 1975 Chvátal result the paper uses
as background, with `source_fidelity: UNCHECKED`, `acceptance_basis: DECLARED_BACKGROUND`,
`statement_alignment: PENDING` and `formalization_mode: EXPLICIT_THEOREM_PARAMETER` — assumed in
Lean, not proved, and declared rather than hidden.

**The bottleneck to verifying a 2026 quantum-information paper is a 1970s graph-theory theorem
that has never been frozen from its primary source and aligned to the conventions in use.** That
is the shortest available argument for the project, and the example says it rather than the
speaker.

An interactive reader for this closure exists at `schema v0.2/host_probe/evidence/blocked-theorem.html`.

## 4. Components to build

### C1 — Response path and `TASK_BINDING` (2–3 h)

Parse the model reply strictly, reject duplicate keys and unknown fields, stage the original
response bytes before any parsing, run the existing cross-object checks, resolve locators, then
commit through the existing store. Add the `TASK_BINDING` layer: output `operation` matches the
Task, every input alias referenced was actually supplied, and `target_ids` resolves as the
operation requires.

### C2 — CLI (2 h)

`prepare` / `run` / `status` over C1, in the shape `handoff.py` already proved: freeze inputs,
persist intent before calling, pin response bytes before committing, idempotent replay, and
cross-process read-back.

### C3 — One real model call (the milestone)

A bounded `paper.extract` over the theorem environments of `draft.tex`, under any one provider,
producing a `COMMITTED` run whose seven check layers each carry a real report, and whose
`issues` disclose the unread remainder as `UNREAD_SCOPE`. This is v0.2's own stated next
acceptance case, and it is the only thing on this list that turns the protocol from executable
to executed.

If two credentials are available, run the same frozen Task twice under different providers and
retain both original responses. That makes Principle 2 — shared format, differing content —
observable rather than asserted, and it is the first time `X-07` becomes runnable.

### C4 — Chain folders with honest provenance (1.5 h)

Six per-paper folders: the five chain papers that already have V1-format artifacts under
`agents/`, plus one for the Chvátal node that has **no folder because the 1975 source was never
frozen**. Every file carries a provenance label: `PRODUCED_UNDER_V0.2_CONTRACT`,
`CARRIED_FORWARD_FROM_V1_PILOT`, or `NOT_PRODUCED`.

The empty folder is the best `ls` in the talk: the chain has six roots, five have folders, and
the one that does not is the one blocking the main theorem.

### C5 — Neo4j import (optional, 1 h)

`schema v0.2/host_probe/evidence/projection.cypher` already exists: 29 nodes, 45 edges,
deterministic projection keys per `STORAGE.md`, every edge carrying
`disposition: 'ORACLE_CANDIDATE'`. Neo4j is not installed (no Docker; Homebrew is present).
Import is a one-hour task if 09-21 has slack, and the file is import-ready either way.

Projecting the full 74-node DAG is **not** attempted: `claim-dag.json` contains neither
`content_hash` nor `revision`, and its four-value edge vocabulary is not the eight-value v0.0
`BindingKind`. Both the surveyor and its skeptic priced that at roughly 120 hours of scientific
content work — authoring ~203 new records, each needing a comparison array that `STORAGE.md`
forbids auto-filling. It is labelled `NOT_PROJECTED` and left there.

### C6 — The talk (1 day)

Structure in §8; vision content in §9.

### 8.1 The cost slide

Worth its own slide, and not only for reassurance: it turns the whole proposal from abstract into
arithmetic, and it pre-empts the question this audience will have anyway — whether any of this is
economically absurd.

**Two scopes, kept apart, or the number is wrong by a factor of four.** v0.2's Lamport→Lean branch
runs on **one selected MathClaim** by design; there is no whole-paper autoformalization operation.
So "formalize the paper" and "formalize every claim in it" are different questions.

For arXiv:2607.26154v1 the claim count is not a guess: the MathClaimIR registry holds **25**
records for it. Taking the measured spec prefix of 58,155 bytes (~14.5k tokens, identical on
every call) and the per-operation call model from `docs/IDEAS-BACKLOG.md` item 5:

| Scope | Calls | Input | Output | At $5 / $25 per MTok |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — extract, dependency on all 25 claims, formalize **one** | 66 | 1.17 M | 146 k | **≈ $9.5 per paper** |
| **B** — the same, formalizing **all 25** | 210 | 3.93 M | 770 k | **≈ $39 per paper** |

Both figures are estimates in two respects that must be said on the slide: token counts come from
a four-characters-per-token approximation rather than the provider's counting endpoint, and the
rate card must be looked up on the day rather than quoted from this document.

**The most useful number on the slide is not the price.** It is that **82% of all input in scope A
is the same 14.5k-token specification prefix, sent 66 times.** The largest single lever is
therefore free — cache the prefix, no quality traded — and it comes before any tiering decision.
The adapter already renders the prompt in cache-friendly order (instruction, then specification,
then the varying brief and sources) but declares no cache breakpoints, so at present that 82% is
paid in full.

**Then effort tiering, which is the interesting half.** Reasoning effort is the first
quality-trading lever after the free wins, and the four operations do not deserve equal spend:

| Operation | Shape of the work | Effort to start from |
|---|---|---|
| `paper.extract` | extraction and classification over supplied text | low / medium |
| `dependency.search` | comparing conditions and intended use | medium |
| `autoformalization.lamport` | structuring a proof | high |
| `autoformalization.lean` | writing code that must compile, iteratively | xhigh / max |

The last row is where effort repays most and where the cost concentrates — in scope B,
`autoformalization.lean` alone is over half the bill, and its **iteration count**, not its
per-call price, is what decides the total. That is the first quantity to measure. The 20-minute
compile of `relaxed_vertices_affineSpan` during the gap analysis is not evidence for it: that
target's answer was already known.

Say the honest part out loud. **An effort allocation is itself a claim about where the difficulty
lies, and it is testable.** If `low` on extraction holds quality, the task was easier than the
architecture assumed — and that is a result, not a saving. Which is why this is measured per
route on real requests rather than set globally, and why cost is judged per *completed task*
rather than per request: a cheaper call that needs three more iterations is not cheaper.

Two implementation items follow from this section and are small enough to name:

1. **Cache breakpoints.** Split the rendered prompt into content blocks and mark the stable
   prefix, rather than sending one string. Biggest lever, no quality cost.
2. **Per-operation effort.** `generation.effort` is currently one value per profile; it belongs
   per operation. Keep it provider-neutral: the profile declares what the vendor supports, and
   the run records what actually happened — effort used, and cache-read tokens — so the slide's
   numbers come from receipts next time instead of from a model.


## 9. Vision — four directions

**A standing statement, repeated on every slide in this section.** The distance between the
current implementation and any of these four directions is large. None is a roadmap item with a
date. Each requires sustained work by a community rather than by one project, and each is stated
with what exists today and what is missing, so the gap is visible rather than implied.

### 9.1 Reuse

**The vision.** When proving a new theorem, determine which already-verified upstream
declarations can be extended to it. Two consequences beyond convenience.

*Mathematical theorems and physical semantics become tightly coupled.* Because the six-axis
separation forbids a Lean theorem from ever standing in for a physical statement, the physical
content — model assumptions, approximation regime, the observable actually measured — must be
written down explicitly instead of living between the lines.

*The unit of formalization changes.* The prevailing practice is to formalize one isolated paper,
producing thousands of lines of Lean that no human reads or navigates. Here what a human reads is
a **dependency chain**, built layer by layer, each rung carrying a name, its conditions, its
source anchor and its state; Lean declarations are evidence attached to rungs. This reframes the
relationship to existing formalization libraries: **they provide the terrain, this provides the
map.** What those projects lack is not tooling but an answer to which one should be proved next
and what proving it unlocks.

**What exists today.** Three imports each with an `assumption_match` review.
`AgtXIvStabilizerness` audits three reused graph-theory declarations inside a
quantum-information target. `root_standardization_priority` records a build order with written
reasons for five roots. The closure reader renders the chain for one paper.

**Distance.** Large. No automated search for reusable upstream declarations exists;
`dependency.search` is specified, and the only executing path before v0.2 was a bounded local
bibliography scan. Matching an upstream declaration's conditions to a new target is a human
judgement. `SemanticContract` is specified and has no producer.

**What a community would have to do.** Maintain a shared, versioned registry of verified
declarations carrying machine-readable conditions. Agree conventions for weight, sign and
normalization alignment across fields. Reach coverage at which "is there something upstream" has
a non-trivial answer more often than not.

### 9.2 Knowledge increment

**The vision.** Measure what a new theorem actually contributes to the knowledge base, against a
fixed baseline, without claiming world-first.

**What exists today.** `agent.json` has a `local_delta` section; the Lean status is literally
`TARGET_LOCAL_DELTA_KERNEL_CHECKED`; the DAG separates inherited roots from new claims, so "what
is new here" is readable from structure. The Delta Agent contract states its rule directly:
compare against a fixed baseline, do not claim world-first.

**Distance.** Large. The Delta checker currently only requires the comparison set and the
assigned targets to intersect — gap `G-07`. Identity assembly that would make an independence
claim meaningful is not wired — `G-08`. No comparison has been run on a real pair.
"Contribution" at present means structural difference within one frozen batch, which is not
scientific significance. And `delta.compare` is not in v0.2's mandatory startup path at all — it
remains a v0.1 specification awaiting a versioned adapter.

**What a community would have to do.** Agree what a baseline means within a field. Assemble a
corpus large enough that the baseline is not arbitrary. Find reviewers willing to sign delta
assessments and be accountable for them.

### 9.3 Knowledge discovery

**The vision.** Three questions. Which theorems matter in a field. By what route were they
obtained. And whether the pattern of connections can be learned well enough to propose new
theorems worth proving.

**What exists today.** Importance is computable from the graph: out-degree within this DAG gives
`claim:frustration-graph` 10 and `root:gottesman-stabilizer-formalism` 9.
`root_standardization_priority` records five priorities with reasons. Edges carry a `type`, and
multi-premise inference is an explicit `via_step` node rather than a lost hyperedge, so "by what
route" is recoverable. 74 nodes span 13 branches.

**Distance.** Very large, and the three questions are not equally far. One paper's DAG is not a
field, and degree within a single hand-built DAG is not importance. The second question is partly
answered by existing structure. The third — learning connections to generate new theorems — is an
**open research question, not a roadmap item.** Its precondition is that enough claims are
verified and condition-carrying that the connections are worth learning from; learning from
candidate edges would be learning from noise. This is precisely why the graph must remain derived
and must never become a second write path: the trustworthiness of a connection cannot exceed that
of the records it connects.

**What a community would have to do.** Build many papers' DAGs against a common contract.
Establish cross-paper node identity. Publish honest negative results about which connections did
not generalize.

### 9.4 Structured transparency, against black-box exploration and compute asymmetry

**The vision.** Reduce the black-box character and the randomness of AI-driven knowledge
exploration, and prevent the ability to *check* a result from becoming a function of capital.

**Context (September 2026).** OpenAI reported a proof concerning the Navier–Stokes equations
produced by roughly 10,000 agents running for 88 hours on an internal model, and stated it does
not intend to claim the Millennium Prize. A credit dispute followed. Tristan Buckmaster (NYU)
states that OpenAI offered him sole authorship conditional on removing Levent Alpöge, an
Anthropic researcher, and that he refused; shortly before the announcement, Buckmaster and Alpöge
had released a solution to the closely related Euler equations. OpenAI denies wrongdoing, states
that no person or model there had seen the other work and that its solution differs, while
conceding that de-identified product data may have improved its models. These are the parties'
stated positions and should be presented as such.

**The structural point, not a slogan.** If checking a result requires owning comparable compute,
verification itself becomes a function of capital. A byte-anchored, structured record decouples
checking from compute ownership. State the cost honestly: re-checking needs a one-time Mathlib
cache download of a few gigabytes over a few minutes; after that, re-checking this result is
84 seconds on one laptop. A few gigabytes and a few minutes is within reach of any graduate
student; ten thousand agents for 88 hours is not. That is a difference in kind, and it is the
accurate version.

§8.1 is this argument in arithmetic, which is why the cost slide comes first: on the order of ten
dollars to process a paper, against ten thousand agents for eighty-eight hours to produce one
proof nobody outside can re-derive. The asymmetry is not rhetorical, and it is not even close.

**What exists today, and this is where the newest work lands.** A `Task` freezes its visible
inputs; a `run` records real execution attribution; every claim carries byte anchors and content
hashes; fields such as `source_fidelity: UNCHECKED` force the system to declare what it has *not*
checked. The Delta Agent's "do not claim world-first" rule was written before the dispute — a
design principle predating the event.

And as of 2026-09-17 the protocol is **provider-neutral in running code, not only in principle**.
The whole specification renders to 58,155 bytes — about 14.5k tokens — so any model with an
ordinary context window can receive the entire protocol and be held to it by the same checker.
Four profiles render a real request today, including one for a model the reader runs on their own
machine. The contract does not privilege a vendor: `model_profile` is a pinned descriptor, and
`provider` is data in the receipt. **A student with a local model and a laptop can produce a
record that is checkable by exactly the same rules as one produced by a frontier model.** Whether
the content is as good is then an empirical question, and Principle 2 requires that any
difference be visible rather than concealed.

**Distance.** Large. The host executes no operation end to end yet, and until C3 lands no model
has run under these contracts at all. Independent identity assembly, evidence gates and formal
admission are specified and unimplemented (`G-08`, `G-09`). And nothing here constrains a system
that declines to adopt such contracts: adoption is a question of community norms, not technique.

**What a community would have to do.** Journals, referees or funders would have to ask for
records of this kind. Enough groups would have to adopt them that a result arriving without a
traceable chain reads as incomplete rather than as normal.
